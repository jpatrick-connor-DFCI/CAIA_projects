## Methods

**Transfer and analysis population.** We applied the federated platinum models trained without DFCI to the DFCI-PROFILE ADT cohort using frozen preprocessing and coefficients. Analyses used landmarks of 0, 90 and 180 days, with day 180 primary for every model family, including the checklists (as in Figure 6). Outcomes were measured from the landmark; patients with the endpoint at or before the landmark were excluded. Scores were flagged at or above the within-landmark 80th percentile, retaining ties; checklists used their frozen flag rules. We estimated uncertainty using 1,000 patient bootstrap draws and suppressed strata with fewer than 10 events. [Describe the handling of missing annotation labels and confirm the model horizon and selection.]

**AVPC criteria.** We tabulated each qualifying criterion and C7-only cases among patients with AVPC. Criteria are not mutually exclusive. CRPC dates came from the mCRPC labels; we report their agreement with the CRPC dates in the AVPC labels. [After verifying source evidence, describe whether criteria reuse laboratory or Gleason inputs.]

**Lead time.** For AVPC and NEPC, we compared cases diagnosed 0–180, 181–365, 366–730 and 731–1,825 days after the landmark with noncases followed event-free for at least 730 days or to the end of the case band, whichever was later, using case–control AUC. As a sensitivity analysis, we used all observed noncases. The follow-up rule selects for follow-up and death can preclude observation; these AUCs are descriptive and may be biased by censoring and competing death. Among eventual flagged cases we summarized time from landmark to documented diagnosis and CRPC and platinum milestones, alongside sensitivity, flagged fraction, and two-year Kaplan–Meier risk. We excluded cases known to have month/year-only dates in a sensitivity analysis; AVPC diagnosis precision is not available in the current label export, so CRPC-date precision served as a proxy for AVPC. Cumulative flags were tabulated across available landmark frames.

**Specificity.** In patients with CRPC after the day-180 landmark, the primary case definition was AVPC or NEPC, whichever came first, by 730 days after CRPC; controls had neither phenotype and at least 730 days' follow-up after CRPC. We excluded patients with insufficient follow-up. Secondary analyses used AVPC and NEPC separately, excluding patients with only the other phenotype. We repeated the analysis at 365 days and among patients with CRPC before or at the landmark. Clinical predictors were age, log(1 + PSA) at CRPC, log(1 + days from ADT to CRPC), metastatic status at CRPC (dated metastasis by CRPC; a later metastasis or non-metastatic label; or metastatic without a recorded date), Gleason category and ADT intent. We compared score-only AUC with five-fold out-of-fold penalized clinical and clinical-plus-score logistic predictions, with a paired patient bootstrap conditional on those predictions. We estimated an adjusted odds ratio and likelihood-ratio test using separate unpenalized in-sample models. In a sensitivity analysis we removed cases that met AVPC only through C7, retaining composite cases that were also NEPC cases. [Describe missing-data imputation and complete-case exclusions from the output.]

We separately applied the frozen model on endpoint-specific frames for platinum, AVPC, NEPC, docetaxel started on or after CRPC, cabazitaxel, radium-223, Lu-177-PSMA and death, with any docetaxel start and first ARPI start as sensitivity comparators. Hazard ratios were expressed per SD of a single patient-level score distribution, so they share a scale across endpoints. We report endpoint-specific cohorts and the intersection of patients eligible for all primary endpoints. Cause-specific analyses of the first new therapy class (platinum, taxane, radium-223, Lu-177-PSMA or ARPI; a class was new only if first started after the landmark) censored at a different first new class and excluded same-day first-class ties. These associations are descriptive and do not by themselves establish specificity. For exploratory CRPC-anchored scoring, we rebuilt pre-CRPC features with landmark 0; the model was originally trained on the ADT clock. Events dated on or before the CRPC date cannot be scored on this clock, and we report their number.

**Calibration and subgroups.** For platinum, we used the exported elastic-net Breslow baseline and linear predictors to estimate 1-, 2- and 3-year risks. We compared observed Kaplan–Meier risk with mean predicted risk, estimated an unstandardized Cox calibration slope, IPCW Brier score and KM-referenced scaled Brier score, and plotted two-year calibration by predicted-risk decile, pooling adjacent deciles to at least 10 events. We also re-estimated the DFCI baseline with coefficients fixed and re-estimated both slope and baseline; these locally updated estimates are not external validation. We assessed the direct checklist at its model horizon, pooling adjacent point totals to at least 10 events. Its generalized (rounded-threshold) version changes item counts, so its original probability calibrator was not valid; we report observed risk only for that version. The XGBoost margin had no exported absolute-risk baseline. We assessed age, ADT intent, metastatic status at anchor, anchor year, Gleason, sequencing, and, when a verified source was supplied, race and ethnicity. Subgroup hazard ratios were expressed per SD of the full cohort's score, and heterogeneity was assessed with score-by-group Cox interactions on the same scale. Kaplan–Meier analyses treat competing death as censoring and do not estimate competing-risk cumulative incidence.
